# Hazardous Materials in YOUR Cabinet Shop

## Who this lesson is for

Every Southbrook employee, with extra attention from anyone on the shop
floor. By the end of this lesson you can name every significant chemical
and physical hazard in your normal day, recognise the pictograms it
carries, and know what to do if it lands on you, catches fire, or spills.
This is about **our specific products**, not a generic list.

## What you'll see in the shop

Below are the nine hazards grouped by where they show up in workflow.
For each: **what it is**, **hazard type + pictograms**, **PPE**, and
**what to do if exposed or spilled**.

## Recognition + response

### 1. Wood dust

**What it is.** Particulate from sawing, routing, sanding, edge-banding
trim-back. Includes hardwood dust (oak, maple, walnut, cherry, cedar)
and engineered-product dust (MDF, particleboard, melamine).

**Hazard + pictograms.** **Combustible dust** — fine dust suspended in
air ignites readily; cyclones and ducts are explosion-rated for a
reason. **Respiratory carcinogen** — oak, walnut, cedar, beech are
classified as nasal/sinus carcinogens with long-term exposure. **MDF
dust** contains formaldehyde resin binders (respiratory irritant). Some
species cause skin sensitization. Pictograms on packaged MDF: Health
Hazard, Exclamation Mark.

**PPE.** Safety glasses, **N95 minimum** for sanding (higher-rated
respirator for prolonged work or when dust collection is down), long
sleeves, hearing protection at sanders. Wash hands before eating.

**If exposed.** Eye: flush. Skin reaction: wash, note the species, tell
supervisor. Heavy inhalation: fresh air, doctor if breathing affected.

**If spilled / accumulated.** **Never use compressed air** (re-suspends
dust, explosion risk). HEPA vacuum or sweep with dust-suppressing
compound. Keep collection bin from overflowing.

### 2. Solvent-based finishes (lacquers, stains, conversion varnishes)

**What it is.** Nitrocellulose and pre-cat lacquers, post-cat (acid-cured)
conversion varnishes, oil-based stains, solvent dyes.

**Hazard + pictograms.** Flame (flash points often below 25 °C),
Exclamation Mark (irritation, sensitization), Health Hazard (toluene,
xylene, glycol ethers — chronic liver/kidney/CNS effects).

**PPE.** **Half-face or full-face respirator with organic-vapour
cartridges** (cartridge type is on SDS Section 8). **Nitrile or
solvent-rated chemical gloves** — leather is not solvent-rated. Safety
glasses, face shield for high-volume spray.

**If exposed.** Skin: wash with soap and water, do not use solvent. Eye:
15-minute eye-wash, medical attention. Inhalation: fresh air, supervisor
notified. Ingestion: do **not** induce vomiting, call Poison Control,
bring SDS.

**If spilled.** Small (under ~1 L): eliminate ignition sources,
ventilate, absorb with clay/vermiculite, dispose in approved metal
waste container. **Large: evacuate, alert supervisor.** Never mop,
never use water, no electrical switches in vapour area.

### 3. Acid catalysts (for conversion varnishes)

**What it is.** Catalysts (often para-toluene sulfonic acid) mixed into
post-cat varnishes to cure them. Sold in small bottles.

**Hazard + pictograms.** **Corrosion** (skin/eye burns), sometimes
**Skull and Crossbones** (acute toxicity), **Health Hazard**
(respiratory sensitization).

**PPE.** **Butyl or neoprene gloves** (not standard nitrile — check
SDS), **face shield**, chemical apron, respirator with cartridge
specified on the SDS. **Eye-wash within 10 seconds reach before opening
the bottle.**

**If exposed.** Skin: 15-minute flush, remove contaminated clothing,
seek medical attention. Eye: 15-minute eye-wash, immediate medical
attention. Ingestion: do **not** induce vomiting, call Poison Control,
bring SDS.

**If spilled.** Small: neutralize per SDS Section 6 (often sodium
bicarbonate slurry), absorb, dispose as hazardous waste. Large:
evacuate, alert supervisor, do not approach without full PPE.

### 4. Contact cement / edge-banding adhesives

**What it is.** Solvent-based contact cement (laminate work), hot-melt
EVA or PUR adhesives (edge banding).

**Hazard + pictograms.** Solvent contact cement: Flame, Health Hazard,
Exclamation Mark. Hot melt: primarily thermal burn; some carry
Exclamation Mark.

**PPE.** Contact cement: nitrile gloves, eye protection, organic-vapour
respirator in unventilated areas. Hot melt: heat-resistant gloves near
applicator, eye protection.

**If exposed.** Contact-cement skin contact: soap and water before it
cures, never use solvent. Hot-melt burn: cool with running water 10–15
min, **do not peel the hardened adhesive off** (takes skin with it),
medical attention if burn is larger than a quarter.

**If spilled.** Contact cement: same as solvent finishes (flammable
liquid procedure). Hot melt: let cool and solidify, scrape, dispose.

### 5. PVA / yellow wood glue

**What it is.** Polyvinyl acetate (Titebond II/III etc.). The everyday
assembly glue.

**Hazard + pictograms.** Low overall, but skin sensitization and eye
irritation with daily contact. Exclamation Mark (some products carry
none).

**PPE.** Nitrile gloves, safety glasses, eye-wash within reach.

**If exposed.** Skin: soap and water. Eye: 15-minute flush, medical
attention if irritation persists.

**If spilled.** Wipe with water before it cures; peel/scrape after.

### 6. Lacquer thinner and mineral spirits

**What it is.** Solvent blends used for thinning, cleanup, gun-flushing.
Lacquer thinner is the aggressive one (acetone / toluene / MEK blend);
mineral spirits is the milder petroleum distillate.

**Hazard + pictograms.** Flame (very low flash point on lacquer
thinner), Exclamation Mark (defatting, irritation), Health Hazard
(chronic inhalation). **Aspiration hazard** if swallowed — chemical
pneumonia.

**PPE.** Nitrile or solvent-rated gloves, safety glasses, organic-vapour
respirator for sustained use.

**If exposed.** Skin: soap and water — **never use more solvent to
clean off solvent**. Eye: 15-minute flush. Inhalation: fresh air,
medical if dizziness persists. Ingestion: do **not** induce vomiting,
Poison Control, bring SDS.

**If spilled.** Same flammable-liquid protocol as item 2.

### 7. Cleaning solvents (denatured alcohol, acetone, isopropanol)

**What it is.** Surface-prep and hand-cleanup solvents.

**Hazard + pictograms.** Flame (acetone is particularly volatile),
Exclamation Mark.

**PPE.** Nitrile gloves, safety glasses, ventilation.

**If exposed / spilled.** Same flammable-liquid protocol as lacquer
thinner.

### 8. Compressed air and pneumatic systems

**What it is.** Shop air at 90–120 psi plus the pneumatic tools and
spray guns it drives.

**Hazard.** **Physical, not chemical** — but serious. Air injected
through skin can cause embolism (life-threatening). Eye injury from
debris when blowing off a workpiece. Hearing damage from sustained
exhaust noise.

**PPE.** Safety glasses **always** when using compressed air, hearing
protection, gloves.

**If exposed.** Air-injection injury: **call 911 even if the entry
wound looks small**. Do not wait and see. Eye debris: eye-wash, medical
attention if anything is embedded.

### 9. Forklift propane and forklift batteries

**What it is.** LPG cylinders for propane forklifts; lead-acid
batteries for electric forklifts.

**Hazard + pictograms.** Propane: Flame, Gas Cylinder (flammable +
pressure + asphyxiant). Lead-acid battery: Corrosion (sulfuric acid),
Flame (hydrogen gas during charging).

**PPE.** Propane changeover: safety glasses, gloves — **specific
forklift-propane certification required first**. Battery charging /
fluid check: safety glasses, face shield, acid-resistant apron and
gloves; do not bypass charging-area ventilation.

**If exposed.** Propane leak (smell of gas): leave the area, no
ignition sources, alert supervisor. Battery-acid skin/eye contact:
immediate 15-minute flush, medical attention.

**If spilled.** Battery acid: neutralize with sodium bicarbonate
slurry per SDS, hazardous-waste disposal. Propane leak: evacuate,
ventilate, no electrical switches.

## Common mistakes + how to recover

**"My respirator is on the wrong cartridge — but it's the same brand."**
Cartridges are colour-coded for hazard class. An organic-vapour cartridge
will not stop ammonia; a dust filter will not stop solvent vapour. Check
the cartridge against SDS Section 8 every time.

**"I used solvent to clean lacquer off my hands."** Now you have solvent
absorbing into your skin. Soap and water for skin cleanup, not solvent.

**"The dust collector is making a weird noise but I have panels to cut."**
Stop the job. A failing dust collector is a combustible-dust explosion
risk. Tell your supervisor.

**"I'll only use the contact cement for 30 seconds — I don't need the
respirator."** A 30-second exposure can trigger a sensitization reaction.
Sensitization is not reversible. Wear the respirator every time.

## Quiz (5 questions, applied)

**1.** You're sanding solid walnut for two hours on the random-orbit
sander with dust collection running. Do you need a respirator?

> Yes — N95 minimum. Walnut dust is a recognized carcinogen and
> sensitizer. Dust collection reduces but does not eliminate exposure.

**2.** A coworker spills about 2 L of lacquer thinner on the finishing
room floor and reaches for a mop. What do you tell them?

> Stop — do not mop (spreads vapour, fire risk). Eliminate ignition
> sources, do not flip electrical switches. Ventilate. Get the spill
> kit, absorb with clay/vermiculite (not water). Dispose in the metal
> waste container. Tell the supervisor and log the incident.

**3.** You're about to mix acid catalyst into a conversion varnish for
the first time. What is your first PPE step and what must be within
reach?

> Face shield + chemical apron + butyl or neoprene gloves (not standard
> nitrile — confirm in SDS Section 8) + respirator with cartridge from
> the SDS. The **eye-wash station and emergency shower must be within
> 10 seconds reach** before you open the bottle.

**4.** A nail gun misfires and you feel a sting on your palm. The
puncture is barely visible and there is no nail. What do you do?

> Treat as compressed-air injection injury and **call 911**. Do not
> wait — air under the skin can cause embolism or serious tissue
> damage. Notify your supervisor.

**5.** Hot-melt adhesive drips onto your hand at the edge bander and
hardens. What do you do?

> Cold running water 10–15 minutes. **Do not peel the adhesive off** —
> it takes skin with it. First-aid responder decides whether to leave
> it until it releases naturally or whether medical attention is
> needed.
