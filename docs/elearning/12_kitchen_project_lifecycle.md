# Kitchen Project Lifecycle — The Seven-State Machine

## Who this lesson is for

You're the project manager or designer who actually drives a kitchen
through its design conversation, customer approval, and handoff to the
shop floor. Course 2 lesson 2.1 gave you the planner's view ("these
three records describe the same kitchen"). This lesson is the inside
view of one of those records — `sb.kitchen.project` — and the seven
states it moves through. You need to know which button to press, in
what order, when each state requires a specific artifact, and what
fires downstream when you flip a state.

Get the state machine wrong and you'll either jam a half-designed
project into production, or you'll discover at install time that the
customer never actually approved anything in the system.

## Where this lives on the site

For the designer:

> **Sales → Kitchen Workspace → Projects**

The list view of `sb.kitchen.project` records. Filter by your own
designer user; sort by `date_created desc`. Click a project to open
the form.

For the project manager:

> **Sales → Kitchen Workspace → Projects → My Active Projects**

The same model, filtered to your assigned partners and states
`designing / awaiting_customer / approved`. The PM doesn't typically
work `draft` or `done` records — those are designer / archive
concerns.

The Kitchen Jobs board (Kitchen Ops → Kitchen Jobs) is the
**downstream** view — it shows `project.task` spines, not
`sb.kitchen.project` records. The two records bridge at the
`sale_order_id` field; the project doesn't appear on Kitchen Jobs
until its sale order is confirmed (see "what fires downstream" below).

## What your screen shows

On the `sb.kitchen.project` form:

- **Name** (`name`) — required, free text. The designer writes
  something human like "Smith Kitchen — Walnut Shaker".
- **Code** (`code`) — auto-generated by sequence
  (`KP/2026/000123`), readonly. This is the stable identifier across
  the lifecycle.
- **State** (`state`) — the seven-state Selection (`draft`,
  `designing`, `awaiting_customer`, `approved`, `in_production`,
  `done`, `cancelled`). Tracked, so every flip lands on chatter.
- **Theme** (`theme`) — `signature` / `elegance` / `contemporary` /
  `contractor`. Aligned to the configurator's series choice. Drives
  what door styles and finishes the configurator offers downstream.
- **Date created** (`date_created`) — when the designer first hit
  Create. Readonly.
- **Target completion** (`date_target`) — the designer's internal
  estimate for when the kitchen ships. Editable.
- **Date completed** (`date_completed`) — written when state moves
  to `done`. Readonly.
- **Customer** (`partner_id`) — the `res.partner` who's paying.
  Required. Tracked.
- **CRM opportunity** (`opportunity_id`) — link to the `crm.lead`
  this project came from. Optional but encouraged for sales-funnel
  reporting.
- **Designer / Salesperson** (`salesperson_id`) — defaults to the
  user who created the project.
- **Design options** (`design_option_ids`) — the One2many to
  `sb.kitchen.design.option`. The A/B/C alternatives.
- **Selected option** (`selected_design_option_id`) — computed from
  `design_option_ids.is_selected`. Exactly one must be True before
  the customer can approve.
- **AI room analysis** (`ai_analysis_id`) — Many2one to
  `sb.kitchen.ai.analysis`. The Gemini analysis output for this
  room. Critical: its `confirmed_by_human` flag gates downstream
  configurator runs (see "behind the scenes").
- **Appliances** (`appliance_ids`) — One2many to `sb.kitchen.appliance`.
  Each carries its own `confirmed_by_human` flag.
- **Approvals** (`approval_ids`) — `sb.kitchen.approval` records,
  one per gate (concept / design / engineering / production-release).
- **Sale order** (`sale_order_id`) — written when the configurator
  generates a quote from this project.

## The seven-state machine — what each state means

The states form a directed acyclic graph enforced by
`VALID_TRANSITIONS` in
`southbrook_kitchen_workspace/models/sb_kitchen_project.py`. Any
transition off-graph raises `UserError`.

### State 1: `draft`

The designer created the record. Customer is set; theme is probably
set; nothing else. No design options yet. Allowed next states:
`designing`, `cancelled`. **Who triggers**: the designer, via
**Start Designing** button (`action_start_designing`). **What fires
downstream**: nothing — no quote, no task, no MO. This is private to
the designer.

### State 2: `designing`

The designer is iterating with the customer on photos, AI analysis,
design options. Allowed next states: `awaiting_customer`, `cancelled`.
**Who triggers**: the designer, via **Submit to Customer**
(`action_submit_to_customer`). **Required artifact**: at least one
`sb.kitchen.design.option` must exist (enforced by
`_require_at_least_one_design_option`). **What fires downstream**:
sends the `email_template_concepts_ready` mail template (best-effort,
logged-not-raised on failure). Nothing in production land yet.

### State 3: `awaiting_customer`

The customer is reviewing options. The designer is hands-off; the
record is waiting on a customer email or call. Allowed next states:
`approved` (yes), `designing` (back to drawing board), `cancelled`.
**Who triggers**: the designer, via **Customer Approves**
(`action_customer_approves`) when the customer says yes, or **Back
to Designing** when the customer wants changes. **Required artifact**:
exactly one `design_option_ids.is_selected = True` (enforced by
`_require_selected_design_option`). **What fires downstream**: sends
`email_template_design_approved`. Customer-facing — they get the
"thanks, your design is approved, here comes a quote" email.

### State 4: `approved`

Design is locked. The configurator now uses
`selected_design_option_id` as the canonical input. A quote /
`sale.order` is typically generated at this point and linked via
`sale_order_id`. Allowed next states: `in_production`, `cancelled`.
**Who triggers**: typically the PM (not the designer), via **Release
to Production** (`action_release_to_production`) AFTER the customer
confirms the sale.order (pays the deposit, signs the contract).
**What fires downstream**: sends `email_template_released_to_production`.
THIS IS THE BRIDGE INTO KITCHEN OPS — the sale order's
`action_confirm` override (in `southbrook_premium_orchestration`)
creates the `project.task` spine, which lands on the Kitchen Jobs
board.

### State 5: `in_production`

The shop floor is working on the cabinets. The
`project.task` spine is being driven through readiness, release, and
scheduling lessons (12.3, 12.4, 12.5). Allowed next states: `done`,
`cancelled`. **Who triggers**: the PM, via **Mark Done**
(`action_done`) once the cabinets ship and install completes.
**What fires downstream**: sends `email_template_project_done`,
sets `date_completed = today`.

### State 6: `done`

The project is closed. The cabinets are installed; the customer is
(hopefully) happy. No further state transitions allowed except
manual archival outside the state machine. **Who triggers**: terminal
state, nobody triggers a move out of it.

### State 7: `cancelled`

Anything from `draft` through `in_production` can land here.
**Intentional gap**: there's no automated email on cancellation —
that conversation should be a phone call, not a templated message.
**Who triggers**: any role, via **Cancel** (`action_cancel`). Terminal.

## The VALID_TRANSITIONS map

For reference (from `models/sb_kitchen_project.py`):

```python
VALID_TRANSITIONS = {
    ("draft", "designing"),
    ("draft", "cancelled"),
    ("designing", "awaiting_customer"),
    ("designing", "cancelled"),
    ("awaiting_customer", "approved"),
    ("awaiting_customer", "designing"),  # back to drawing board
    ("awaiting_customer", "cancelled"),
    ("approved", "in_production"),
    ("approved", "cancelled"),
    ("in_production", "done"),
    ("in_production", "cancelled"),
}
```

Attempting any transition not in this set raises `UserError` with the
list of valid next states. The state can't be force-set even from
the API; `action_set_state` is the only entry point and it validates.

## Your daily flow

### The designer (per project)

1. **Create** the project under Sales → Kitchen Workspace → Projects.
   Set customer + theme. State = `draft`.
2. Take photos of the customer's room, attach them via chatter. Run
   AI room analysis (Gemini-backed) — that creates an
   `sb.kitchen.ai.analysis` record. Open it, validate the
   detected dimensions, **set `confirmed_by_human = True`** — this is
   GAP-02 / Module 6, the configurator REFUSES to run otherwise.
3. List the appliances the customer has (stove, fridge, sink). For
   each, set `confirmed_by_human = True` after confirming with the
   customer.
4. Click **Start Designing** → state moves to `designing`.
5. Author 1-3 `sb.kitchen.design.option` records (A/B/C). Each one
   can have its own placement_data, sketches, notes.
6. Click **Submit to Customer** → state moves to `awaiting_customer`.
   The customer gets the "concepts ready" email.
7. The customer comes back: either picks one option (sets
   `is_selected = True` on it) and you click **Customer Approves**,
   or asks for changes and you click **Back to Designing**.
8. Once **Customer Approves** fires, state = `approved`. The
   configurator now reads the selected option as the canonical
   design input. Quote generation begins.

### The PM (per project)

1. The designer hands off in state `approved` with a linked
   `sale.order` in `draft` or `sent` state.
2. Confirm the sale order (separate Odoo Sales action — this triggers
   the `action_confirm` override that creates the `project.task`
   spine).
3. Click **Release to Production** on the kitchen project → state
   moves to `in_production`. This is a SALES-side bookkeeping flag;
   the actual shop floor release happens through the engineering
   gate covered in lesson 12.4.
4. Monitor the Kitchen Jobs board (which now shows the
   `project.task` spine) until the cabinets ship and install. Once
   verified, click **Mark Done** → state = `done`.

## What fires downstream at each state change

This is the "if I click this, what happens elsewhere?" map every PM
needs:

| Trigger | Source state | Target state | Downstream effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| `action_start_designing` | draft | designing | None (private to designer) |
| `action_submit_to_customer` | designing | awaiting_customer | `email_template_concepts_ready` sent to customer |
| `action_customer_approves` | awaiting_customer | approved | `email_template_design_approved` sent. Quote can now be generated via configurator. |
| `action_release_to_production` | approved | in_production | `email_template_released_to_production` sent. **Sales-side flag only** — the actual production release happens via the engineering gate (lesson 12.4). The `project.task` spine that drives Kitchen Ops was created by `sale.order.action_confirm` separately. |
| `action_done` | in_production | done | `email_template_project_done` sent. `date_completed` set. Project goes off the active list. |
| `action_cancel` | any (per transitions) | cancelled | **No email** — intentional. |

The most-missed nuance: clicking `action_release_to_production` on
the `sb.kitchen.project` does **not** flip the `project.task`'s
release state. They're independent. See Course 2 lesson 2.1 quiz
question 4 — they typically agree but can disagree when the engineer
hasn't yet approved a specific MO.

## What the AI confirmation gate enforces

The `is_ready_for_config_engine()` method on `sb.kitchen.project` is
called BEFORE the configurator runs. It returns `True` only when:

1. `ai_analysis_id` is set, AND
2. `ai_analysis_id.confirmed_by_human = True`, AND
3. EVERY `appliance_ids.confirmed_by_human = True`.

If any of the three fails, the configurator refuses to generate a
quote and the designer sees an error popup. This is the
human-in-the-loop guarantee: the AI's measurements never reach
production without a designer eyeballing them. It's GAP-02 from the
init doc and one of the few hard refuse-to-proceed gates in the
whole platform.

## Common mistakes + how to recover

**"I tried to click Customer Approves but got 'no design option is
selected'."**

The customer told you they like Option B, but nobody flipped
`is_selected = True` on it. Open the design options tab on the
project, edit Option B, tick the box, save. The compute on
`selected_design_option_id` updates immediately; click Customer
Approves again.

**"The configurator refused to generate a quote with 'project not
ready for config engine'."**

You skipped the AI / appliance confirmation. Open `ai_analysis_id`,
verify dimensions, set `confirmed_by_human = True`. Then walk every
appliance and confirm each. The gate clears the moment all three
conditions are met; no further action needed.

**"I tried to push state from `designing` directly to `approved` via
the dev shell and got UserError."**

That's the state machine enforcing `VALID_TRANSITIONS`. You CAN'T
skip `awaiting_customer` — it's there to capture the actual customer
yes/no moment. The right path is `designing → awaiting_customer →
approved`. If you're testing, set `is_selected = True` on a design
option, then call `action_submit_to_customer`, then
`action_customer_approves`.

**"I clicked Release to Production on the project but the
project.task on Kitchen Jobs didn't move to release=ready."**

These are independent. `sb.kitchen.project.state = in_production` is
the SALES-side flag — "this kitchen is past the design conversation
and into production accounting." The task's release state is
controlled by the ENG01 engineer flipping the five booleans (lesson
12.4). They CAN agree but they don't have to.

**"The customer wants to come back to designing after I clicked
Approved."**

You can't transition `approved → designing` (it's not in
`VALID_TRANSITIONS`). The choices are: (a) move to `cancelled` and
start a fresh project (preserves the audit trail but feels
heavy-handed); (b) keep state at `approved` and treat the changes as
post-approval design tweaks (typical for minor changes — door style
swap, accessory adds). For major redesigns, option (a) is the right
call; the new project can reference the old via chatter for context.

**"I clicked Mark Done but the cabinets haven't actually shipped
yet."**

You can't undo to `in_production` (no transition). Recovery options
are limited: either accept the early flip and rely on chatter for
the truth, or use the API to write `state = in_production` and
clear `date_completed` (requires admin access — `action_set_state`
won't let you). Better: don't click Mark Done until installation is
verified.

## What the system is doing behind the scenes

`sb.kitchen.project` is defined in
`southbrook_kitchen_workspace/models/sb_kitchen_project.py`. It
inherits `mail.thread` + `mail.activity.mixin` so every state flip
lands on chatter with the user who triggered it. The state field has
`tracking=True` so the chatter shows the from/to transitions; this
is your audit trail.

`action_set_state(new_state)` is the single entry point. Every
convenience button (`action_start_designing`,
`action_submit_to_customer`, etc.) ultimately calls it. The method
walks `self`, checks `(current, new_state) in VALID_TRANSITIONS`,
raises `UserError` with the list of valid next states if not, then
writes the new state plus side-effect fields (`date_completed` on
`done`).

The lifecycle email helper `_send_lifecycle_email` is **best-effort,
logged-not-raised**: if the mail template doesn't exist or the send
fails, the state transition still completes. The chain is
deliberate — the state machine is the truth, email is a side-effect
that can be re-sent manually if it fails. Templates live in
`data/mail_templates.xml`.

The `is_ready_for_config_engine()` gate is the only place in this
addon where downstream code is blocked. The configurator
(`product.config.session`) calls it before generating a
`product.config.session` line and refuses if False. No other code
path checks it — if you bypass the configurator's check, the gate
doesn't fire.

The bridge to `project.task` is NOT in this model — it's in
`southbrook_premium_orchestration/models/sale_order.py`. When the
sale.order is confirmed, the `action_confirm` override creates a
`project.task` spine. The `sb.kitchen.project` is the **input** to
that flow, not the producer. This is why moving `sb.kitchen.project`
to `in_production` doesn't create a task — the sale order's
confirmation does.

## Quiz (5 questions, applied)

**1.** A designer is iterating with a customer who keeps changing
their mind. The project is in `awaiting_customer`. Customer asks for
a fourth option. The designer wants to add a new
`sb.kitchen.design.option` without going through the state machine.
Is that OK?

> Yes — adding a design option doesn't require a state flip. Open
> the design options tab, hit Create, fill in option D. The
> `selected_design_option_id` recompute fires on save. If the new
> option is the one the customer chooses, mark it
> `is_selected = True` (which auto-clears the others via the
> compute), then click Customer Approves. The state machine only
> guards the seven states, not the underlying data.

**2.** A PM tries to release a kitchen project to production but the
underlying sale.order is still in `draft` state. What happens?

> Two things are independent. `action_release_to_production` on the
> kitchen project will run successfully — it only checks the
> kitchen project's state machine, not the sale order's state.
> State moves to `in_production`. BUT the Kitchen Jobs board won't
> show a `project.task` until the sale order is confirmed, because
> the spine creator runs in `sale.order.action_confirm`. Recovery:
> confirm the sale order in Sales → Quotations. The
> `project.task` lands; the kitchen project is already where you
> want it.

**3.** A customer cancels mid-production. The kitchen project is
`in_production`, the cabinets are half-made, the sale order is
`sale`. What's the right sequence?

> (1) Click Cancel on the kitchen project. State moves to
> `cancelled`. No automated email — pick up the phone. (2) Open the
> sale order. Mark it as Cancelled (Odoo Sales action). The
> in-flight MOs need separate cancellation in Manufacturing — go
> through each linked `mrp.production` and click Cancel. (3) Open
> the linked `project.task` (find it via the chatter on the sale
> order or via `x_southbrook_project_task_id`). The cron will
> recompute its readiness as blocked overnight, or you can manually
> archive it. (4) Inform the floor — they may already have material
> on the cut line that needs to be scrapped or rerouted.

**4.** The AI room analysis on a project shows the kitchen's width
as 4.2m but the designer measured 4.0m on site. What does the
designer do?

> Open the `sb.kitchen.ai.analysis` record. Edit the dimension
> fields to the designer's measurements (4.0m). THEN flip
> `confirmed_by_human = True`. The flag means "I, a human, validated
> these numbers" — flipping it without correcting first is the
> failure mode the gate exists to prevent. The configurator then
> uses the corrected 4.0m for cabinet sizing.

**5.** A designer asks "can I just delete a project in `draft` state
to clean up tests?"

> Yes — `draft` projects haven't fired any downstream signals (no
> emails, no quotes, no tasks), so deleting them is clean. Odoo
> standard `unlink` works. The chatter and any attached photos are
> deleted too. For `cancelled` projects with downstream artifacts
> (sale orders, tasks), archival is safer than deletion — use the
> archive action on the form, which sets `active = False` without
> losing the audit trail.
